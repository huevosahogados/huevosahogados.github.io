The idea

Blocked repetition, running the same piece until it goes well, builds short-term fluency, but the next day you will be closer to where you started then where yiu ended up. By selecting only the challanging parts of all of your reportoire, breaking them down into small, maneagable chunks, and randomizing the order in which you practtice them, making sure to only spend a few minutes on each before moving on builds Rotating between pieces before any of them feel solid builds long-term retention.

The discomfort is the mechanism.

Molly Gebrian's work on retrieval practice and Noah Kageyama's writing at Bulletproof Musician both point the same direction: the practice that feels hardest in the room holds longest.

Interleave is built around that principle.

The practice that feels hardest in the room is often the practice that holds longest.

How it works

Repertoire

Your pieces, your way

Add pieces, passages, or exercises as image items — a photo of your score, a PDF page, a screenshot. Name it. Build up as many as you have.

Sessions

Randomized queue, on a timer

Interleave builds a randomized queue and walks you through it. Each item arrives without warning. When time is up, you move on.

Weighting

Harder pieces come up more

After each item, tap − (harder) or + (easier). Pieces you're struggling with appear more often. Pieces in good shape fade toward maintenance.

Annotations

Mark up the score

Draw directly on the score image — circles, arrows, fingerings. Annotations persist between sessions.

Rest cards

Built-in breaks

Optional prompts between items: a breath, a hand stretch, a posture reset. You choose which categories appear.

Compás

Metronome included

40 to 208 BPM, tap tempo, per-beat accent control. Runs alongside your session.

Nothing to install.
Runs in any mobile browser.

No account needed. Your repertoire stays on your device. Works on phones, tablets, and e-ink readers. Useful for students too — just send the link. A native app is in the works.
