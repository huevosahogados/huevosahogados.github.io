The approach

Theory and technique in sevice of musicality.

Although each student brings something different to the lesson, the common element is clearing out confusion and finding the easy way - for them - to approach each problem. I don't teach from a method book, I draw from a wide range of sources and create my own materials, often for each student.

We will place particular emphasis on practice techniques that build solid foundations and accelerate learning.

Music theory

Music theory should make music easier, not harder. It is best when it relates to the music we are working on. Rather than learning complex and esoteric theoretical structures, we look at what needs to be learned to make the music at hand make sense logically.

Technique

Addressing the root of technical issues, not the symptom, so progress comes more easily.

Musicality

Learning to really listen makes phrasing, dynamics, and momentum natural and more musical. Our aim is to make music, not to play notes.

Practice methods

How to practice is more important than how much we practuce. We work on strategies that compound over time.

Specialties

Rooted in Latin America, open to everything.

My deepest background is in Latin American popular and folk music — choro, samba, tango, and related traditions. I bring that harmonic and rhythmic richness into lessons across all genres.

Most of my students are working in jazz, pop, rock, folk, or classical repertoire. Whatever you're into, we'll find a way in.
